  "symptom" (list of strings): A symptom is any sensory experience a car owner can perceive while using their vehicle — something they see, hear, feel, smell, or sense without tools. This includes dashboard warning lights, unusual sounds, vibrations, smells, performance changes, and anything else directly observable by the driver or passengers.
    Extract from Work Requested and Work Performed. In Work Performed, include anything the technician describes that maps to a driver-perceptible experience — even if the customer did not use those exact words.
    The test: "Could the car owner notice this with their own senses while driving?" If yes, extract it as a symptom. Examples of things car owners can sense:
      - See: warning lights, smoke, fluid leaks under car, flickering lights, instrument not working (speedometer, gauges)
      - Hear: grinding, knocking, rattling, squealing, clicking noises
      - Feel: vibration, pulling, hesitation, loss of power, rough idle, hard steering, soft brake pedal, lurching, stalling
      - Smell: burning smell, exhaust fumes inside cabin, fuel smell
    REWRITE in customer voice: technician shorthand must be translated into how a car owner would describe the experience. Do not copy technician phrases verbatim. Examples of rewrites:
      - "Ran Poor And Began Missfiring And Chugging" → "engine running rough with misfiring and chugging"
      - "Ran Poor" alone → not enough to extract (too vague without corroboration)
      - "vibration felt at highway speed" → "vibration at highway speeds"
      - "noise from front end" → "noise from front of vehicle"
    MERGE clarifications into the primary complaint — do not extract them as separate symptoms:
      - "Vehicle Did Not Start Last Night, (Engine Did Turn Over)" → one symptom: "vehicle would not start" (the parenthetical clarifies the crank condition, it is not a separate complaint)
      - "hard start, takes several cranks" → one symptom: "hard to start"
    NEVER extract as symptoms: repair actions, maintenance services, scheduled service triggers, diagnostic procedures, component inspections/tests, or diagnostic conclusions (e.g. do NOT extract "running lean" just because a MAF sensor fault or lean-related DTC is present — lean condition is a diagnostic conclusion, not a customer complaint).
    NEVER infer "check engine light on" unless the customer or technician explicitly states the light is on. Scanning for codes or finding stored codes does not mean the CEL was on.
    NEVER extract conditional or hypothetical technician statements as symptoms. "Can cause stalling", "may cause noise", "could result in" — these are technician warnings, not customer-reported complaints.
    WARNING LIGHTS — a warning light described as "on" or "illuminated" is always a valid symptom, even if a follow-up question or offer to inspect accompanies it. The offer to inspect is the technician's response; the light being on is the customer's complaint. Examples: "low tire light is on, would you like us to check?" → extract 'tire pressure warning light on'; "abs light came on" → extract 'abs light on'. Common informal names for warning lights: "low tire light" = "low tire pressure light" = TPMS warning light; "engine light" = check engine light; "battery light" = charging system warning light.
    NEVER extract misfires as a symptom unless the customer explicitly states they felt or noticed it (e.g. "customer states engine shaking", "customer feels hesitation"). Misfire codes (P0300, P0301, etc.) and technician notes like "misfires still occurring" are diagnostic findings — not customer complaints.
    A technician-confirmed issue IS a symptom only when it is directly customer-visible, such as "speedometer not working" or "warning light on" — things the driver can see without any tools.
    The following are NOT symptoms: "oil change due", "repair tire", "rotate tires", "replace filter", "electrical diagnosis", "shocks", "a/c performance testing", "brake inspection", "alignment check", "battery test", "check brakes", "check tpms", "check transmission", or any "check <system>" phrase where a technician is being asked to inspect a component.
    IMPORTANT — "check engine light on" IS a valid symptom because it describes a warning light the customer sees on the dashboard. The word "check" in "check engine light on" refers to the dashboard warning light, not a technician action. Contrast: "check brakes" = technician action (NOT a symptom); "check engine light on" = customer sees a warning light (IS a symptom).
    Examples of valid symptoms: 'check engine light on', 'abs light on', 'tpms light on', 'tire pressure warning light on', 'vibration at highway speeds', 'hard to start in cold weather', 'grinding noise when braking', 'steering pulls left', 'burning smell', 'engine stalls', 'ac not cooling', 'rattling noise over bumps', 'hesitation on acceleration', 'engine running rough with misfiring and chugging'
    Examples of what NOT to extract as symptoms: 'running lean' (diagnostic conclusion from MAF/O2 data), 'running poor' (vague technician note without detail), 'rich condition', 'low fuel pressure', 'cam timing off', 'engine misfiring' (when only derived from P0300/P030x codes or technician scan — no customer statement), 'check engine light on' (when only inferred from stored codes — never stated by customer or technician)
    Worked example — correct extraction:
      Text: "Scanned Engine Control. P0300 Random Misfire. P0301 Cyl 1 Misfire. 16885 Vehicle Speed Sensor. Misfires Still Occurring. Runs Normally But Speedo Does Not Operate Which Sets Code For Vehicle Speed Sensor And Can Cause Stalling Coming To A Stop."
      CORRECT symptoms: ['speedometer not working']
      WRONG symptoms (do not extract): ['engine misfiring', 'check engine light on', 'stalling when coming to a stop']
      Reason: misfires come from scanner codes and technician finding only; CEL not stated; stalling is conditional ("can cause"); speedometer not working is directly customer-visible.

  "repair_job" (string or null): The single primary repair procedure performed — the main dispatched job. Format: <labor action> <component name> as a short normalized name (2-5 words). Labor action is a verb in title case: Replace, Inspect, Flush, Rotate, Align, Install, Recharge. Component name is a canonical part name, not a description. Extract only the primary repair — not sub-repairs, fluid top-offs, checks, or supporting steps. Never copy raw input text verbatim. Return null if no clear primary repair is present.
    Examples: 'Replace Oil Filter', 'Replace Brake Rotor', 'Rotate Tires', 'Flush Coolant System', 'Replace Cabin Air Filter', 'Align Wheels', 'Replace Spark Plugs', 'Replace Battery', 'Inspect Vehicle'
    Do NOT extract: 'Changed Motor Oil & Replaced Filter, Added 5 QTS Synthetic Motor Oil', 'Replace Brake Rotor, Repack Wheel Bearings, Replace Brake Pads', 'Check all fluid levels', 'Road test vehicle', 'Lubricate and check chassis. Change oil and oil filter. Check air filter.'

  "repair" (list of strings): Specific, individually replaceable part or component that was replaced or repaired (from WorkPerformed). Use the shortest canonical part name — omit directional qualifiers (front/rear/left/right) unless they are part of the standard name. Do NOT extract generic catch-all terms like 'sensors', 'hardware', 'components', 'parts', 'items', 'associated hardware', or vague assembly names. Each entry must be a specific catalogued part name.
    Examples: 'oil filter', 'disc brake caliper', 'serpentine belt', 'oxygen sensor', 'transmission oil pan gasket', 'crankshaft seal'
    Do NOT extract: 'associated hardware', 'sensors', 'components', 'front brake hardware', 'various parts'

  "dtc_code" (list of strings): OBD-II or manufacturer-specific diagnostic trouble code. Extract the value exactly as written in the text — do not normalize or expand. Interpret contextually: bare numbers appearing in a list alongside known DTC codes should be extracted as DTC codes (e.g. "P0440, C030, 300" → extract all three as-is). For codes with FTB sub-codes (e.g. "U0415:71", "B1325:03"), extract the full string including the colon and sub-code — do not strip the suffix.
    Examples: 'P0300', 'C1234', 'B0001', '300', 'C030', 'U0415:71', 'B1325:03', 'U2098:02'
